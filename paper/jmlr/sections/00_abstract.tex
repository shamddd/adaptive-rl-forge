\begin{abstract}
Standard foundation model training relies on a rigid sequential paradigm: Next-Token Prediction (NTP) pre-training, followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and finally Reinforcement Learning (RL). While recent work demonstrates that RL excursions during pre-training can expand model distribution coverage, fixed or manual excursion schedules fail to account for dynamic checkpoint readiness. In this paper, we investigate the underlying mechanisms of reinforcement learning plasticity across intermediate pre-training checkpoints. We discover that measurable pre-RL state signals—specifically gradient alignment between NTP loss and task rewards, policy entropy, and baseline reasoning accuracy—predict subsequent RL performance gains before compute is expended. Leveraging these diagnostic signals, we propose the \textbf{Capability-Aware Reinforcement Learning Scheduler (CARLS)}, a dynamic controller that dynamically allocates compute among NTP, SFT, and RL objectives during training. Empirical evaluations across multiple random seeds and scale configurations demonstrate that CARLS achieves superior compute-normalized performance and capability retention while consuming up to 37\% fewer training FLOPs compared to standard sequential pipelines.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{reinforcement learning, large language models, training dynamics, optimization, compute allocation}
